A part you hummed into your phone is a start. The bass line you add on Tuesday, and the harmony you only got right on the ninth try, are the rest of it. They all have to line up.

A session is what holds them. Start one from a recording you already have, or from nothing at all. The session plays back while you record, so what you played against is what you hear afterwards, and each pass lands on its own track at the point in time where you played it.

## A track is a thing, not just a layer

- Every track carries its own level, its own mute and its own solo.
- Rename a track from either device. Colour and instrument icon are set on a computer and show up everywhere.
- On a computer a track also has a pan control and a level meter beside its lane.
- Lock a track and nothing can move it or record over it.

## One input, or several

On a phone or tablet you arm one track and record into it. On a computer you can arm several at once, each input landing on its own track, with every lane drawing as you play.

The click and a one-bar count-in are a tap away, and the session's tempo and time signature set the grid that everything lines up to.
