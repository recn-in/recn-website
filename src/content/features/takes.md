The fourth pass is usually the good one. Sometimes it was the second, and you only work that out an hour later.

Recording onto a track that already has audio replaces what was there. The old take is not deleted. It stays with the track as a thin strip along the bottom of the lane, and the track's menu lists every take the track holds. Pick an earlier one and the track plays that instead.

## Nothing you played is thrown away

- Exactly one take is active on a track. That is the one you hear, and the one an export renders.
- The others stay with the track, in the order you recorded them.
- Trimming is not destructive either. Pull a clip's edge back out and the audio returns.

## Round and round

With the loop armed, record keeps wrapping instead of stopping at the end of the region, and each lap is committed as its own take. Four times round the chorus leaves you four takes to choose between, rather than one long file to cut up afterwards.

Takes work the same way on a phone or tablet. Recording replaces, and the take you replaced is waiting in the track's menu.
